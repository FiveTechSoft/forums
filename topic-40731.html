The Most Important Thing: Choosing the Right Problem

Many people ask about tools, methods, or resources. But the real question is:
Why do you choose a specific problem?
And how do you turn it into a solution that truly addresses customer needs?
This is where the core of entrepreneurship lies.

The Art of Entrepreneurship
Principle 1: Distribution First
Traditionally, the focus was on building a great product first, then figuring out how to reach customers. But today, the game has changed.
You must assume that someone else is already working on a similar idea. That’s why distribution—your ability to access and engage your target audience—is critical.
Here’s my advice: Choose a niche you already understand.
If you’re already part of that community:

Code: Select all Collapse
You know their pain points.
You understand their needs.
You know where they spend their time.
You know how to connect with them.

That is your competitive advantage. Big corporations or outsiders don’t have this insight.

Principle 2: Know When to Stop Building
This is a common challenge for entrepreneurs.
There’s always a temptation to add more:

Code: Select all Collapse
More features.
More complexity.
More "just one more thing."

In the past, limited resources or high costs naturally forced discipline. But now, with tools and resources more accessible than ever, you have to set the limits.
Define your scope:

"This is my minimal viable solution. This is what proves I can solve the problem."
This discipline is what separates successful projects from endless tinkering.

What’s your experience? Have you struggled with choosing the right problem or knowing when to stop building? How do you stay focused?

Why I Chose a Custom-Built Solution for the WINHOTEL Community Hub

When I look at the future of small software companies, I see two topics becoming increasingly important:

  1. Customer Retention

Acquiring new customers is becoming more time-consuming and expensive.

At the same time, the greatest long-term value is often created through satisfied existing customers.

That is why we need ways to stay connected with our customers, answer questions quickly, share experiences, and build a stronger connection within the user community.

  1. Continuous Product Improvement

Software is no longer a finished product.

It evolves continuously.

To do that, we need to understand:

where users encounter problems,
which features are missing,
which workflows can be improved,
and which ideas come directly from practical experience.

Direct dialogue with users therefore becomes an important part of product development.

Why Not Use Existing Forum Software?

At this point, I asked myself:

Should I use existing forum software, or should I develop my own solution?

The answer surprised even me:

I'm not actually looking for a forum.

I'm looking for a meeting place for customer engagement and product development.
