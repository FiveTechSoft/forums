What keeps us (still) writing code? And what could this forum be today?

Posted: Tue Nov 24, 2026 08:22 AM

Hello friends,

The forum used to be like a neighborhood pub or coffee shop: drop in briefly, grab a quick coffee, see a few familiar faces, toss a problem across the table, or ask for some advice.

Today, the "Harbour 4" thread has almost 8,000 views and practically no replies. 8,000 readers, a handful of voices. That isn't indifference, but it shows something clear: people are reading, and not enough people are talking.

Especially now, when so much runs through AI tools, you read everywhere that the human element is regaining massive importance—not because of the sheer amount of information, but because of the quality of the exchange. Today, AI often gets things 100% right, yet for our specific situation it often lacks the full context—the context that we have, having accompanied our grown projects and each other for decades. Precisely this shared knowledge and the feeling of making the right choices for the future together is what makes the difference.

With AI, we code differently today: faster and often bolder. Still, the core concepts remain crucial: architecture, clean structures, maintainability, and how to break down tasks so AI can solve them well. At the same time, we face very practical questions about the continuity of our work: how do we successfully market new applications and modernizations? And how do we handle succession or handover for our own projects built over decades? That is exactly what we should be discussing:

Practical AI tips: Which tool, which prompt, and which workflow actually works well with xBase, Harbour, and FiveWin?

Concepts: How do you structure projects so AI can meaningfully assist? What context do you provide?

Pitfalls: Where does AI produce nonsense—for example with legacy xBase code—and how do you catch it?

Tackling legacy systems: Refactoring, documentation, testing. What can finally be accomplished with AI that sat untouched for years?

Future, marketing & succession: How do you position and market new applications to existing or new clients—and how do you cleanly prepare a decades-long life's work for a successor?

One more thought: we are a generation experiencing this transition with full hands-on experience, and we can help shape it. From Clipper and DOS all the way to AI, we have seen it all. That is a tremendous wealth of knowledge that belongs in the conversation—and it is precisely this human experience that connects us.

My suggestion to bring the forum back to life: Let’s start a permanent, shared regulars' thread: "What did I build this week—or what is currently on my mind?" No matter how small the topic is—whether a clever AI workflow, a marketing idea, thoughts on succession, or simply a quick hello from daily development life. A place to drop by without needing a big occasion and feel the value of a genuine community.

How do you see this—and what is keeping you busiest right now? Best regards, Otto
